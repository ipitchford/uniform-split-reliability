# Source and novelty audit

Publication intake date: 18 October 2026. This is a bounded source comparison,
not exhaustive novelty certification. The publication workflow inspected the
supplied dated novelty report and primary Romero 2025 statements (Theorems 10,
12 and balloon definition), plus Csikvári 2011 Theorems 2.2 and 2.6(b) and its
historical attribution of multigraph extensions to Brown–Colbourn–Devitt 1993.

Searches included uniformly most split reliable graphs/balloons, split reliability
cone graphs, the 1993 transformation article and the 1996 least-reliability article.
No matching resolution of the residual cone family was found in that bounded
search. Absence from these results is not proof of novelty.

The complete 1993 paper was not available through the inspected publisher route;
no claim that the setwise exchange condition or multigraph generality is new is
made. The 1996 bibliographic record/abstract confirms the dense threshold, but
the publication intake did not obtain its full proof. The theorem is therefore
explicitly imported with this source-access limitation, not claimed as a fresh
proof verification. The paper's own new arguments and interval extension are
presented in full, so private research notes are not necessary to read them.

The editor additionally inspected Romero, Universal Reliability Bounds for
Sparse Networks, Section III, PDF page 4, which explicitly states the Petingi
dense threshold e >= (n-1)(n-2)/2+1 and the exact balloon construction:
https://redi.anii.org.uy/jspui/bitstream/20.500.12381/648/1/10%20(1).pdf .
This corroborates the imported theorem's statement through a primary later paper,
but is not inspection of the original 1996 proof. Brown–Mol, arXiv:1703.10566,
Definition 3.1 was also inspected and confirms the 2017 split-reliability
definition. These are source-to-claim checks, not merely bibliographic matches.

The analytic interval extension was compared with the existing candidate's
endpoint statements. A bounded search did not find its exact statement; the
cut/tree-bound method is elementary, and no exhaustive priority claim follows.
Correctness, originality, significance and computational repeatability remain
separate questions. Full bibliographic details appear in the manuscript.
