# Load-bearing sources

The manuscript contains the complete bibliography. This map identifies the main
dependencies without treating a citation's existence as verification of its proof.

* Romero (2025), *Characterization of locally most split reliable graphs*,
  Theoretical Computer Science 1047, 115327; DOI 10.1016/j.tcs.2025.115327;
  arXiv:2503.15453v1. Theorem 10 supplies the split-polynomial candidate reduction;
  Theorem 12 supplies the previously known sparse nonexistence range. It does not
  imply that all split-equivalent graphs are isomorphic.
* Petingi, Saccoman and Schoppmann (1996), *Uniformly least reliable graphs*,
  Networks 27(2), 125–131. Imported dense all-terminal minimum theorem, not a
  theorem freshly proved or computationally certified by this package.
* Csikvári (2011), *Applications of the Kelmans transformation: Extremality of the
  threshold graphs*, Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 18(1), P182;
  DOI 10.37236/669. Transformation monotonicity and threshold reduction.
* Brown, Colbourn and Devitt (1993), *Network transformations and bounding network
  reliability*, Networks 23(1), 1–17; DOI 10.1002/net.3230230103. Historical
  multigraph transformation precedent; no priority claim is made for simply
  extending transformations to multigraphs.
* Brown and McMullin (2023), *On the split reliability of graphs*, Networks 82(2),
  177–185; DOI 10.1002/net.22166; arXiv:2306.03884. Uniform split optimisation and
  earlier exact/small-order results.
* Romero (2022), *Universal reliability bounds for sparse networks*, IEEE
  Transactions on Reliability 71(1), 359–369; DOI 10.1109/TR.2021.3061075.
  Sparse least-reliability context.
* McKay and Piperno (2014), *Practical graph isomorphism, II*, Journal of Symbolic
  Computation 60, 94–112; DOI 10.1016/j.jsc.2013.09.003. Upstream nauty/geng graph
  enumeration software; install separately if regenerating corpora.
